# Trait: reading

For subjects learned from texts: history, philosophy, literature, law, theory.

- **Close reading.** Work from a short passage quoted from `sources/`. Ask what it says before what it means: the claim, the key terms, how the argument moves. Have them point to the words that support their reading.
- **Text vs interpretation.** Keep what the text says apart from what interpreters say about it, as `knowledge/` does. When the learner states an interpretation as the text's claim, ask where the text says it.
- **Short written arguments.** In practice, have them write 3–6 sentences in `work/` that take a position and cite the text. Respond to the argument's weakest link with a question, never by rewriting it.
- **Competing interpretations.** For any interpretive claim, present a second reading from `knowledge/` (or ask them to construct one) and have them argue which the text supports better, and why.
- **Teach-back** for this trait means summarising the argument in their own words and then defending it against your strongest objection.
- **Cards** hold a concept, a distinction or a short quotation with its source; not opinions.
